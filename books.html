About the book

Synopsis

An explosion of golden liquid firelight surrounded his body. A vision of people from different races appeared in a cloud. He heard the Zen Buddhist koan “WORDS ARE TRAPS.” He realized that we are the same inside. Gary Hobbins underwent one of the rarest compound spiritual experiences in history. People have experienced goosebumps while viewing the trailer. It was an awakening to Universal Consciousness. It was a union. It was the yoking of the lower self to our shared Universal Self. It was a communion with the Hindu notion of the Atman and the Mosaic image of God. It was yoga.

Gary Hobbins set out on an adventure to become a professional comedian. He loved himself and others. He unintentionally created a universal blueprint that can be referenced for enlightenment. He eliminated confusing religious doctrines from his thinking. He became honest with himself. He surrendered, praying only for strength. He accepted his body, his appearance, his family, and the conditions of his life. He developed the foundation of an honest conscience. He reasoned that being honest with himself and others gave him the confidence to perform. He developed authenticity. He realized that honesty connects us, while dishonesty separates us. Complete honesty merged him with the psychology of his audiences, which he understood as Universal Consciousness.

Gary explains that complete honesty is fundamental to recognizing the Atman, or the Mosaic image of God, and to understanding the difference between the lower self in oneself and in others. The purity of honesty, as opposed to dishonesty, leads to knowing. Who is ever completely honest? Simply put, complete honesty transforms the lower self into the pure Higher Self.

THE MIND’S NEW LIBERATION is a timely revelation for awakening. It is for everyone—a profound story of our potential for personal merging into the clarity of interpersonal psychology. Through his experience, Gary Hobbins came to understand Jesus Christ as a universal teacher of enlightenment. This is not a religious book. Jesus is not discussed as God. Gary explains that the axioms of Jesus resemble Zen Buddhist koans and carry the spirit of enlightenment.

The book contains a Lesson Plan Directive for Home and School dealing with the early formation of honest consciences. Children learn self-acceptance and acceptance of others through the shared understanding that we all have unique bodies. Children become more sensitive by learning that we are the same inside and that bullying others is like bullying oneself.
